# Ok, but AI is bullshit right?

As I said before, I think both extreme positions currently in vogue are lacking in nuance. And I think even if you are opposed to AI, being ignorant of it is dangerous. I'm going to try, as succinctly as I can, to convince you that you at least need to take LLMs seriously as an incremental step toward proletarianization of so-called intellectual labor; in other words a meaningful change in the barganing position of the working class, a transfer of value into the hands of the capitalist class. For that reason alone, it's worth understanding why shaming people for using generative AI online is not enough.

## What is AI

"Artificial Intelligence" has become synonymous in recent years with generative neural networks for producing text, images and sound. But it's important to understand that almost *every* breakthrough in computer science was, at one time, heralded as AI. "AI" has no technical definition; it basically just means the most advanced thing we've managed to make computers do. At the moment, that breakthrough is LLMs.

In the past, when people tried to create machine intelligence comparable to human intelligence, the approach was some flavor of Symbollic AI. This consisted of a human programmer defining a set of rules and routines for decision making, analagous to the rules of symbollic logic. An AI of this flavor can perform far more computations than a human can, but it can't know anything about the game that it's human programmers don't also know; it's limited by our knowledge both of chess and of the programming tools we used to build it. Since the 80s, this kind of AI has been smarter than humans when applied to certain narrowly defined tasks, like playing chess; but it struggled with fuzzy inputs like recognizing shapes in an image, as well as very large possibility spaces, like the game Go.

The first inkling to the broader public that a major breakthrough was coming was when an AI, AlphaGo, beat a top-level human Go player for the first time. This AI used a combination of Symbollic AI plus an adversarial neural network which had been trained on thousands of games of professional Go to intuit the right next move. Since then, we've seen an explosion of investment in AI, in the hopes that by increasing our models capacity to intuit based on context which is opaque to its programmers will translate into generalized intelligence, able to solve problems unknown by the people who built it.

The novelty of this technology is that it can get better only by increasing the amount of resources available to it. With all previous forms of AI, more storage and faster chips could only take you so far; the dramatic breakthroughs came because some clever human thought of a better way to model the desired solution, or a faster way to use the compute resources available. While AI benefits from the same kind of breakthroughs, it does not require them; it can improve simply by expanding the amount of computer hours available to it.
